# Circles

A product within the hypothetical **Connectify** ecosystem. Circles matches small groups of students who share interests and books an activity for them, turning online compatibility into real-world friendships.

## Mission and Vision

**Mission:** Make forming new friendships easier by creating structured, low-pressure opportunities for people to meet and build relationships.

**Vision:** A social environment where online connections naturally lead to real-world relationships, built on shared activities, not passive scrolling.

## The Problem

Students are constantly around other people and online all the time, but a lot of them still struggle to make real new friends. It's not that there aren't enough people. It's that there aren't enough comfortable, low-pressure ways to actually connect.

- Students feel awkward going to events alone
- Many avoid approaching established friend groups
- "I'd go if I knew someone there." / "I don't want it to feel forced."
- Existing platforms help people find events but don't make showing up easier

This matters because feeling like you belong affects mental health and academic performance, and without support it's easy for students to stay confined to their existing social circles.

## Who It's For

- **Campus Transitioners**: freshmen, transfer, and international students who want immediate belonging and structured events
- **Busy & Isolated Achievers**: commuters, STEM majors, athletes, and working students who need efficient, small-group interactions
- **Social Plateau Students**: upperclassmen with a stable friend group who want to expand their bubble without disrupting it
- **Socially Hesitant Connectors**: introverts and socially anxious students who want psychological safety and structured formats
- **Coterminal Students**: students misaligned in age/maturity with their undergrad social circle who want intentional community

### Early Validation

We spoke with 13 Stanford students about making new friends:
- **62%** said they avoid events if they don't know someone attending
- **75%** said they would try an app that matched them into a small group with a planned activity

## How It Works (MVP)

1. Participants fill out a short form about their interests (hobbies, personality, schedule, major, etc.)
2. The matching algorithm groups 4-5 compatible people and automatically books an activity (dinner, mini golf, bowling, escape room, etc.)
3. A group chat is created so the Circle can coordinate before meeting up
4. After the activity, participants rate the experience and can share a moment/photo with their Circle

### User Flow

1. **Sign up**: basics (name, year, major), interests, personality sliders, availability/activity preferences
2. **Get matched**: see your Circle's members and the booked activity
3. **Coordinate**: group chat with a conversation starter to break the ice
4. **Meet up**: attend the planned activity
5. **Reflect**: rate the night, share a moment, decide if you'd hang out again

## Why Now

Date Drop has validated demand for structured meetups that reduce the friction of meeting someone new, but it's built for dating. Most students aren't looking for dating; Circles applies the same structured meetup model to friendship and small groups instead.

## Go-to-Market Strategy

1. **Campus Pilots (Months 1-4):** Launch at 3-5 universities (Stanford, Berkeley, UCLA); partner with orientation teams, student government, and orgs to seed users. Key metric: ≥70% of matched groups show up to their activity.
2. **Organic Flywheel (Months 5-8):** Drive signups via word-of-mouth and referral ("Join waitlist at your school"). Key metric: each Circle generates 2-3 new signups.
3. **Monetization (Months 9-12):** Sign 20+ venue partners, launch tiered subscriptions ($99-$499/mo) with a 10-15% affiliate commission on bookings, and launch Sponsored Circles with anchor brands funding activity costs.

## Business Impact

- **Direct revenue:** venue partner subscriptions plus affiliate commissions on bookings; targeting $800K in Year 1
- **Ecosystem value:** post-meetup "Moments" drive organic UGC; pre-meetup chats are hosted in RealTalk
- Directly delivers on Connectify's vision of online connections leading to real-world relationships

## Required Resources

- **Funding:** employee compensation, marketing budget, venue partnership subsidies
- **Team:** 1 PM, 1 EM, frontend/backend engineers, designers, 2 campus ambassadors per school, 1 data analyst
- **Tools:** matching algorithm, group chat infrastructure, cloud platform
- **Partnerships:** campus street teams, venue partners, university organizations

## Learnings

1. A product idea only becomes viable once a sustainable business model emerges alongside the user value.
2. The loudest users aren't necessarily the most important users, especially with a product designed to reduce loneliness.
3. Interest matching matters, but not as much as having an event planned.
